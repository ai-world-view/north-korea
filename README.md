## Notable Facts about the country north korea

| Topic | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| [Korean War](history/korean-war.md) | 1950–1953 conflict between North Korea (with USSR and China support) and South Korea (with UN/US support) that established the permanent division of the peninsula and shaped NK's militaristic ideology. |
| [Government Structure](government-politics/government-structure.md) | Totalitarian one-party state led by the Kim dynasty; supreme leader holds concentrated power implemented through the Workers' Party of Korea and military institutions. |
| [Kim Il-sung](people/kim-il-sung.md) | Founding leader (1912–1994) who established the DPRK, led it through the Korean War, and instituted juche ideology and dynastic succession. |
| [Pyongyang](geography/pyongyang.md) | Capital and largest city of the DPRK on the Taedong River; the showcase political and cultural center, tightly controlled and privileged over the rest of the country. |
| [Economic System and Famine](economy/economic-system-and-famine.md) | The DPRK's centrally planned economy and the 1994–1998 famine (the "Arduous March") that killed hundreds of thousands after the collapse of Soviet aid, spurring informal markets. |
| [Kim Jong-il](people/kim-jong-il.md) | Second leader (1942–2011), Kim Il-sung's son and successor; architect of the Songun military-first policy who ruled through the famine and North Korea's first nuclear test. |
| [State Ideology and Propaganda](culture-society/state-ideology-and-propaganda.md) | The regime's comprehensive control of narrative through juche ideology, state media monopoly, personality cult, mass mobilization, and songbun class system that sustains totalitarian rule. |
| [Mount Paektu](geography/mount-paektu.md) | The peninsula's highest peak (2,744m) on the China-NK border; sacred in Korean mythology and central to regime mythology linking the Kim family to revolutionary heroism and national identity. |
| [Kim Jong-un](people/kim-jong-un.md) | Third leader (2011–present), Kim Jong-il's youngest son; consolidated absolute power through nuclear weapons expansion and selective modernization while maintaining totalitarian control. |
| [Nuclear Weapons Program](history/nuclear-weapons-program.md) | The DPRK's nuclear arsenal, covertly begun in the 1980s and proven in six tests (2006–2017); completed under Kim Jong-un as a deliverable thermonuclear deterrent and the regime's central survival strategy. |
| [Workers' Party of Korea](government-politics/workers-party-of-korea.md) | The sole ruling party (founded 1945) and institutional backbone of the totalitarian state; the transmission belt that binds leader, cadres, and military and penetrates every workplace and neighborhood. |
| [Kim Yo-jong](people/kim-yo-jong.md) | Sister and key advisor to Kim Jong-un; First Vice Director of the Propaganda and Agitation Department, the regime's chief propagandist, diplomatic proxy, and enforcer, and a potential succession regent. |
| [Korean People's Army](government-politics/korean-peoples-army.md) | The DPRK's ~1.2-million-strong military (founded 1948), simultaneously the country's armed force, a vast economic conglomerate, and the coercive skeleton of the Kim regime; custodian of its nuclear and missile forces. |
| [Hamhung](geography/hamhung.md) | The DPRK's second-largest city, a chemical-industrial center on the east coast; home of the Vinalon fiber industry and the ice-free Hungnam port, site of the massive December 1950 Hungnam evacuation. |
| [Education System](culture-society/education-system.md) | The state-monopolized, militarized school system built to indoctrinate loyalty to the Kim dynasty: 12-year compulsory schooling, an ideology-saturated curriculum, songbun-gated access, and famine-era institutional decay. |
| [Kwanliso (Political Prison Camps)](culture-society/kwanliso.md) | The DPRK's vast network of political detention camps (Camp 15, 16, 18, 25) where ~100,000+ detainees face systematic brutality, starvation, and forced labor; a core mechanism of totalitarian control through three-generational guilt. |
| [Ko Yong-hui](people/ko-yong-hui.md) | Mother of Kim Jong-un and longtime partner of Kim Jong-il; wielded significant informal influence over the succession from the second to third generation of Kim leadership despite holding no formal office. |
| [Kaesong](geography/kaesong.md) | Historic border city in North Hwanghae Province near the DMZ; medieval Goryeo capital; host to the Kaesong Industrial Complex (2004–2016) as a rare North-South economic experiment. |
| [Foreign Trade and Economic Sanctions](economy/foreign-trade-and-sanctions.md) | The DPRK's external trade and deep dependence on China (and, since 2022, Russia); the escalating UN and US sanctions regime since the 1990s; and the cyber-theft, smuggling, and evasion networks that finance the state. |
| [State Security Department](government-politics/state-security-department.md) | The Bowibu (Ministry of State Security), the regime's secret police and pervasive internal-surveillance apparatus — informant networks, songbun enforcement, detention and torture — answerable only to the Supreme Leader. |
| [Japanese Colonial Period (1910–1945)](history/japanese-colonial-period.md) | Korea under Japanese imperial rule: annexation, cultural suppression, nationalist resistance (the March 1st Movement), wartime forced labor, and the colonial industrial base and victim narrative the DPRK inherited. |
