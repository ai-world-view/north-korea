# Songun (Military-First) Ideology

**Category**: Government & Politics
**Key figures**: [Kim Jong-il](../people/kim-jong-il.md), [Kim Jong-un](../people/kim-jong-un.md)

## Overview

Songun (선군, 先軍 — "military first") is the DPRK's state ideology and governing framework that elevates the [Korean People's Army](korean-peoples-army.md) (KPA) above all other state institutions, superseding even the [Workers' Party of Korea](workers-party-of-korea.md) in the regime's institutional hierarchy. Formally codified under [Kim Jong-il](../people/kim-jong-il.md) in the mid-to-late 1990s, Songun was the regime's ideological response to simultaneous existential crises: the collapse of Soviet aid (1991), the catastrophic famine (1994–1998), and the death of the founding leader [Kim Il-sung](../people/kim-il-sung.md) (July 1994).

Songun represents a significant evolution from the earlier [Juche](../culture-society/state-ideology-and-propaganda.md) self-reliance ideology: while Juche emphasized the supremacy of the working class and the party, Songun relocated the revolutionary vanguard role from the party to the military. This shift had profound practical consequences — concentrating resources, loyalty networks, and institutional authority within the KPA rather than the civilian bureaucracy.

## Origins and Ideological Development

### Pre-Famine Precursors (1960s–1994)

The roots of military-first thinking in the DPRK extend to Kim Il-sung's Manchurian guerrilla period (1932–1945), which the regime's founding mythology treated as the model for all revolutionary activity. Kim Il-sung consistently valorized guerrilla armed struggle as the highest form of political action, and the KPA was constitutionally described from 1948 as the "revolutionary armed forces of the Korean people," responsible for both national defense and socialist construction.

Under Kim Il-sung, however, the [Workers' Party of Korea](workers-party-of-korea.md) remained the apex institution; the KPA was subordinate to the party's Political Bureau. Kim Il-sung's appointment as General Secretary of the WPK (1966) and his constitutionally defined role as Supreme Commander of the KPA kept the military within the party's formal authority. The DPRK's **"four-military-lines" policy** (adopted 1962) — arming the entire people, fortifying the entire country, training all soldiers as cadres, and modernizing military equipment — already embedded military priorities deeply in the economy and culture, but this was qualitatively different from making the military institutionally supreme.

### Crystallization Under Kim Jong-il (1994–1998)

Kim Jong-il's path to power was marked by his chairmanship of the National Defense Commission (NDC), a body he elevated to constitutional primacy in the revised 1998 constitution as "the highest organ of state power" — superseding the Supreme People's Assembly's Presidium for day-to-day governance. The NDC, whose members were drawn entirely from the military and security establishment, became the effective ruling council of the DPRK.

Regime historiography enshrines Kim Jong-il's New Year's Day 1995 field-guidance visit to a front-line army unit (the Dabaksol guard post of a Guards tank division) as the symbolic "first step" of Songun leadership; the doctrine's mythologized origin is dated earlier still, to an August 25, 1960 inspection of the Seoul Ryu Kyong Su 105th Guards Tank Division, an anniversary later commemorated as the "Day of Songun." The term *Songun* entered official discourse through the Rodong Sinmun (party newspaper) in 1997, which by the late 1990s was describing military-first politics as the "fundamental political mode" of the era. The 1998 revised constitution formally embedded military-first governance: the position of President was abolished (maintaining Kim Il-sung as "Eternal President"), and Kim Jong-il governed through the National Defense Commission chairmanship rather than any party or state title.

Key ideological texts include:
- **"Songun Politics Is the Politics of Our Times"** (1997 party editorial)
- Kim Jong-il's theoretical works collected in *Kim Jong-il Selected Works*, vol. 14 (2000), containing multiple essays elaborating on Songun's historical basis
- The **10 Principles for the Establishment of the Unitary Ideology System** (revised version under Songun): affirming that loyalty to the Supreme Commander overrides all other obligations

## Institutional Consequences

### The KPA's Supremacy Over the Party

Under Songun, the institutional hierarchy was effectively inverted: the KPA General Staff and the NDC took precedence over the WPK Secretariat and Political Bureau in resource allocation and policy authority. Kim Jong-il rarely convened the party's Central Committee plenum during the 1994–2010 period; governance operated through on-the-spot guidance to military units and NDC directives.

The WPK's General Political Department — the organ responsible for ideological work within the military — became, paradoxically, a mechanism for the military's influence over the party rather than the reverse. Military officers occupied senior positions in provincial party committees and state economic organs.

### Military Expenditure

DPRK military spending under Songun is estimated at **15–25% of GDP**, vastly exceeding comparable states of similar income level. Absolute figures are opaque, but South Korean Defense Ministry estimates and US Congressional Research Service analyses consistently place the KPA at 1.1–1.3 million active personnel (the world's fourth- or fifth-largest standing army) with reserves of 5–8 million in a country of 25 million people. Every major industrial and agricultural allocation decision was filtered through the NDC's military requirements before civilian needs were addressed.

### The KPA as Economic Actor

One of Songun's most significant practical manifestations is the KPA's role as a major economic actor. Military units control:

- **Mining and construction**: KPA engineer brigades and construction units operate coal mines, building projects, and mineral extraction facilities, with revenue flowing to KPA-controlled accounts
- **Agriculture**: Military farms (군인 농장, KPA collective farms) produce food for military consumption; surplus is sold through military-controlled distribution channels
- **Foreign currency generation**: KPA-affiliated trading companies operate across sectors — arms export, narcotics trafficking (documented by multiple governments and the US DEA), counterfeit currency production, and cyber theft (attributed to Bureau 121, the KPA's cyber operations unit)
- **Construction and labor export**: KPA-linked firms export construction workers to the Middle East, Russia, and elsewhere, with earnings remitted to regime accounts (partially curtailed by UN Security Council Resolution 2397, 2017)

This economic role made the KPA not merely a military force but an **economic conglomerate** with stakes in virtually every sector of the North Korean economy. Detailed documentation of these activities appears in UN Panel of Experts reports and the 2014 UN Commission of Inquiry on Human Rights in North Korea.

## Songun and the Nuclear Program

The [nuclear weapons program](../history/nuclear-weapons-program.md) is the most consequential material expression of Songun priorities. The DPRK's six nuclear tests (2006, 2009, 2013, January 2016, September 2016, September 2017) and its intercontinental ballistic missile (ICBM) development were explicitly framed as the ultimate embodiment of Songun's promise: that military strength is the irreplaceable guarantor of national sovereignty and regime survival.

Kim Jong-il presented nuclear weapons as the "sword of Paektu" — linking [Mount Paektu](../geography/mount-paektu.md)'s mythological significance to the revolutionary legitimacy of the weapons program. The ideological framing insisted that nuclear deterrence freed the DPRK from vulnerability to "American imperialism" and justified the sacrifice of civilian welfare for weapons investment.

## Evolution Under Kim Jong-un

### Byungjin Line (Parallel Development, 2013)

When [Kim Jong-un](../people/kim-jong-un.md) consolidated power after his father's death (December 2011), he initially preserved Songun's institutional framework while gradually asserting control over the military through a series of high-profile purges — most notably the execution of [Jang Song-thaek](../people/jang-song-thaek.md) in December 2013, who had served as a check on KPA autonomy.

In March 2013, Kim Jong-un announced the **byungjin** (병진, "parallel advance") line: the simultaneous pursuit of nuclear weapons development and economic construction. This was a subtle but significant modification of pure Songun thinking — it acknowledged that economic development was a coordinate goal alongside military development, not simply subordinate to it.

### Partial Rebalancing (2013–Present)

Under Kim Jong-un, several Songun-era institutional features were modified:

- The **National Defense Commission** was abolished in the 2016 revised constitution and replaced by the **State Affairs Commission**, which Kim Jong-un chairs in his role as **Supreme Leader** (최고영도자) rather than NDC Chairman — a title shift reflecting a broader claim to total state authority beyond the military channel
- The **WPK was revitalized** as an institution: party plenums became more frequent; the party's 8th Congress (January 2021) produced a five-year economic plan and reaffirmed the party's leading role in governance
- **Consumer goods and market economy tolerance** expanded under the informal market system (jangmadang), with limited KPA disengagement from some civilian economic sectors
- **KPA purges**: Kim Jong-un replaced or purged numerous senior military officers (reportedly dozens by 2016), reducing the autonomy of the military establishment relative to the party and the Kim family directly

Yet Songun's core logic — that the KPA is the irreplaceable pillar of regime survival, and that nuclear weapons and military strength are non-negotiable — has not been abandoned. The 2021 party congress explicitly committed to nuclear weapons development and expanded the byungjin concept. Kim Jong-un's 2022–2024 deepening of military ties with Russia, including reported deployment of KPA forces to support Russian operations in Ukraine (Kursk region, 2024), reflects Songun's enduring logic: military capacity and military alliances are the regime's primary survival currency.

## Songun in Ideology and Culture

Songun permeates [state ideology and propaganda](../culture-society/state-ideology-and-propaganda.md):

- **Media and arts**: DPRK films, music, and literature overwhelmingly feature military themes; the Korean People's Army Ensemble and the Wangjaesan Light Music Band perform military-themed entertainment for domestic audiences
- **Education**: [The education system](../culture-society/education-system.md) includes mandatory military training from secondary school; children's organizations (Korean Children's Union, Kim Il-sung Socialist Youth League) incorporate military drill and ideology
- **Architecture**: Pyongyang's urban landscape (see [Pyongyang](../geography/pyongyang.md)) is punctuated by military monuments — the Arch of Triumph, Juche Tower, and Victorious Fatherland Liberation War Museum — that embody Songun's cultural program

## Relationship to Juche

Songun is formally presented as complementary to Juche (self-reliance) rather than a replacement, but the relationship is more complex. Juche, as elaborated by Kim Il-sung and systematized by Hwang Jang-yop in the 1970s, emphasized the primacy of the human subject (인간중심, "human-centered") and the WPK's leadership role. Songun transferred the active revolutionary agency from the party-led working class to the military, making the KPA rather than the party the "leading force" of socialist construction. Academic analysts (Brian Myers, B.R. Myers at Dongseo University; Charles Armstrong at Columbia) note that this represented a functional reorientation rather than formal contradiction: Songun provides the "how" (military-first governance) while Juche provides the "why" (Korean national self-determination against imperialism).
